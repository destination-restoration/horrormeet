Filming locations

Where was The Wailing (2016) filmed?

The Wailing (2016) was filmed at Gokseong County, Jeolla, The stranger's house, Yeoun Village, Seokgok-myeon, Gokseong, Guseongjae Pass, Ogok-myeon, Gokseong, Eupnae Police Substation, Gokseong-eup and 6 more. 10 real locations below, each with what was shot there and its place on the map.

Open them on the live map

  • Gokseong County, Jeolla

    Na Hong-jin named his film after this mountain and river county in South Jeolla and shot a large part of it here, out of 97 location days across Korea. Gokseong is a farming county served by its own railway station.

    See it on the map · Source: en.wikipedia.org

  • The stranger's house, Yeoun Village, Seokgok-myeon, Gokseong

    The Japanese stranger's hideout, the house Jong-goo smashes up while shouting at him to leave, is the last house at the far end of this tiny mountain village. The production restored an abandoned house for the shoot and it still stands empty. The national tourism listing says the site is open at all times, but the neighboring homes are private residences, so park at the village hall, walk in quietly and go in daylight.

    See it on the map · Source: ktourmap.com

  • Guseongjae Pass, Ogok-myeon, Gokseong

    The scene where the truck carrying Jong-goo and his friends hits the stranger's body was filmed on this winding mountain pass road. Today it is a popular driving and cycling route with an octagonal pavilion and viewpoint at the top. It is a public road, open year round.

    See it on the map · Source: ktourmap.com

  • Eupnae Police Substation, Gokseong-eup

    This small police substation on Jungang-ro in central Gokseong served as the station where officer Jong-goo works. It is still a working police post. Visitors can see the exterior from the street but should not go inside to sightsee.

    See it on the map · Source: rda.go.kr

  • Gokseong Catholic Church, Gokseong-eup

    The church where Jong-goo meets the priest while trying to uncover the stranger's secret is Gokseong Catholic Church. It is an active parish church in the town center. Visitors can see the grounds respectfully outside of services.

    See it on the map · Source: rda.go.kr

  • Dongsan riverside fishing spot, Dongsan-ri, Gokseong-eup

    The film's opening scene, with the stranger fishing at the water's edge, was shot at the Dongsan fishing spot on the Seomjin River. Navigation apps list it as Dongsan Yuwonji, a quiet riverside recreation area. It is a public riverbank and visitors can go.

    See it on the map · Source: rda.go.kr

  • Gokseong Metasequoia Road, Gokseong-eup

    Jong-goo and his daughter Hyo-jin ride his motorbike down this avenue of tall metasequoia trees. The tree-lined stretch lies on the road into Gokseong-eup from the Namwon side and is a well known scenic drive. It is a public road, so take care with traffic when stopping for photos.

    See it on the map · Source: junsungki.com

  • Docheon Village, Byeonggok-myeon, Hamyang

    The house where the shaman Il-gwang stages his ritual, Jong-goo's family home in the film, is not in Gokseong but in this farming village in Hamyang County, where the crew filmed the day and night ritual scenes over several days. The pin marks the village hall, not the house. The house is a private residence, look from the sidewalk only; the village lanes are public and pleasant to walk.

    See it on the map · Source: rda.go.kr

  • Maewoldae Falls, Geunnam-myeon, Cheorwon

    The stranger's ritual bathing under a waterfall was filmed here in mid September, after the producers could not find a deep enough waterfall in the south of the country. The falls sit on the slopes of Bokgyesan and are one of Cheorwon's listed scenic sights. It is a public hiking destination, open at all times, reached by a rough mountain trail.

    See it on the map · Source: cine21.com

  • Moraejae Pass road, Jinan

    The drone shot of a winding mountain road when the shaman Il-gwang first appears was filmed on the national road over Moraejae Pass. The producer says the team wanted a road that winds like a snake, ringed by layers of mountains. It is a public road and visitors can drive it.

    See it on the map · Source: cine21.com

Can you visit the The Wailing (2016) filming locations?
Many of them, yes. Each location above says whether it is a public place, a private home or no longer standing. Private homes are for looking at from the sidewalk only: never knock, never step onto the property.
