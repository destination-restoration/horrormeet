THE SÉANCE

Brian Skiba · Director · Gunslingers · Rottentail · Dead Man's Hand

Monday, December 7, 7:30 PM, at Landon's place in Laurel Canyon. About ninety minutes at the table.

Brian, here are the questions we have for you. Read them over and send Landon the numbers you love and any you don't want to touch. Nothing is fixed, and we can cut anything afterwards.

The Ouija board is part of the show. A few times we'll ask you a yes-or-no question that you answer only by sliding the planchette, there's a quick lightning round near the end, and at one point you'll spell two letters. We don't share those questions ahead of time. Hold the planchette on your answer for a second so the camera above the board catches it. You get one pass, you can defend one answer after each round, and you can answer out loud any time you'd rather not touch the board.

Anything you can't talk about yet stays off the table. Tell Landon before we roll and it will not come up.

It's casual. We want the real version of how these films get made.

The opening

  • 1You grew up in Phoenix watching B movies off a TV antenna. Which "bad" movie made you a horror kid?
  • 2You studied English at Arizona State, not film. How did a writer end up behind the camera?
  • 3You have named Raimi, Craven, Carpenter and John Waters as your people. What did each of them teach you that you still use?

The horror years

  • 4Blood Moon Rising started as a short in 2007 and became your first feature: werewolves, vampires and zombies in one movie. What did making it teach you that film school never could have?
  • 5What did your first feature really cost you, in money and otherwise?
  • 6Rottentail is a horror comedy about a mutant rabbit-man. What made you say yes to that?
  • 7You lost days on Rottentail to flooding, including a flooded church set. Walk us through the worst day.
  • 8You have said, "If I can do it practically, I will." Which practical effect are you proudest of, and which one fought you hardest?

The day job

  • 9You spent years directing TV thrillers and Christmas movies, and you have called that the day job that pays the bills. What does that work teach a director?
  • 10Is there a Christmas movie of yours with a horror film secretly hiding inside it?

Stars, guns and eighteen days

  • 11The 2nd was your first full action film. What changed for you after that one?
  • 12Gunslingers was shot in eighteen days in Kentucky during tornado season. How do you make a western in eighteen days?
  • 13Nicolas Cage waited beside your set in costume instead of in his trailer. What is he like to direct?
  • 14You have said you run the camera yourself to earn an actor's trust. What happens between you and an actor when you are the one holding it?
  • 15On some films you write, direct, shoot, edit and even compose the score. Which of those jobs would you never give up, and which would you hand off tomorrow?
  • 16Stephen Dorff keeps coming back to work with you. What makes an actor return to a director?

Critics and money

  • 17Your films find their audience at home more than with critics. What do critics miss about the job you are actually doing?
  • 18You have called getting a film made the hardest part. Without naming names, how does a film like yours actually get paid for?

Back to the dead

  • 19You are attached to Twilight of the Dead, George A. Romero's last zombie story. How did that come to you, and what does it mean to a kid who grew up on those films?
  • 20Deprivation, your film about the sleep experiments, is finished and has not come out. What is the story there?
  • 21What is the horror film you have not been allowed to make yet?

Close

  • 22What is next, where should people find your work, and what would you tell someone about to direct their first micro-budget horror film?
