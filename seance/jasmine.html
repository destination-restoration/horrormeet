THE SÉANCE

Jasmine Davis · Julio C. Martínez Valle · Killer Revenge

Sunday October 4, 2026, 4:00 PM. In person, about forty minutes.

Jasmine and Julio, tick the ones you want and send them back. Ignore anything you would rather not get into.

Questions marked JULIO or BOTH are for Julio or for both of you, and the rest are for Jasmine. We do not have to stick to the list on the day, and we can cut anything afterwards.

There are no wrong answers here. The premise of the whole sitting is that you two finished a feature, which almost nobody who starts one ever does.

1. The opening

  • 1Killer Revenge is finished and the final cut is locked. How does that feel?
  • 2For someone who has not seen it yet, what is it about, in your own words?
  • 3You wrote it, directed it and starred in it, and you co-edited and co-scored it with Edgar Sardarian. Which of those jobs did you enjoy most, and which one would you hand to somebody else next time?
  • 4When did you know you were actually going to finish it, rather than hoping?
  • 5Julio How did the two of you meet, and what made you say yes to shooting it?

2. How it got made

  • 6You made this on a micro budget. What does a micro budget actually buy you, and what can it not buy?
  • 7Eighteen people in the cast. How do you get eighteen actors to show up for an indie?
  • 8Both What did people tell you that you could not do with the gear you had, that you went and did anyway?
  • 9Both How big was the crew on your biggest day?
  • 10What is the single thing you could not afford that you found a way around, and how?
  • 11How long was the shoot in total, and how spread out?
  • 12Both Was there a day where you thought it was over? What kept it alive?

3. Behind the camera

  • 13Julio You came up through the camera department: assistant camera, camera operator, still photographer. What did those years teach you that you used on this film?
  • 14Julio What did you shoot Killer Revenge on, and what about the setup would surprise people?
  • 15Julio Horror lives in the dark, and dark is the hardest thing to light cheaply. How do you make darkness scary instead of just muddy?
  • 16Julio There are two cinematographers on the film, you and Bernie Jack. How did you split the work?
  • 17Both Jasmine is in a lot of her own scenes. How did you two work when the director was standing in the frame?
  • 18Julio What is your favorite shot in the film, and how did you get it?
  • 19Julio Is there a shot you would go back and reshoot if you could?
  • 20Julio You also direct, assistant direct and produce. What changes when you move from behind the camera to the director's chair?

4. Directing for the first time

  • 21Nobody teaches you how to give a note. How did you work out how to talk to your actors?
  • 22You were directing and acting in the same scenes. How did you know your own performance was landing?
  • 23What surprised you most about directing that nothing had prepared you for?
  • 24Was there a moment where an actor gave you something better than what you wrote?
  • 25Both Did you storyboard, shot list, or work it out on the day?

5. The edit

  • 26Cutting your own film means killing your own work. What was the hardest thing you cut?
  • 27Did the film get shorter or longer than you planned, and what did that teach you?
  • 28At what point did you stop being the writer and start being the editor?
  • 29Is there a scene that only works because of something that happened in post?

6. Putting it out yourself

  • 30You are putting it out yourself rather than waiting on a distributor. Talk us through that decision.
  • 31What is the upside of self-releasing that filmmakers underrate?
  • 32What would have to be true for you to go the distributor route on the next one?

7. What's next

  • 33You are already writing Killer Revenge 2. What is it about?
  • 34What is the single biggest thing you are doing differently, and what are you keeping exactly the same?
  • 35Both Bigger budget, or the same budget spent smarter? Where does the money go first?
  • 36Both You two have a short film in post together right now. What can you tell us about it?

8. For everyone about to make their first one

  • 37What is the piece of advice you would give someone on day one of their first feature?
  • 38What is the pitfall you walked straight into that you can now see coming a mile off?
  • 39Julio What is the one piece of gear a first-time filmmaker should actually spend money on, and what can they skip?
  • 40Both What did you spend money on that was worth every cent, and what was not?
  • 41What do people get wrong about how hard the first one is?

9. The fun end

  • 42If someone handed you ten times the budget tomorrow, what is the first thing you would spend it on?
  • 43Which horror actor would you most want to direct, and what would you put them through?
  • 44Julio Which horror film has the cinematography you would most like to steal from?
  • 45Both What horror film made you want to make horror films?
  • 46Both Practical effects or digital, and why?
  • 47What scares you personally, that you have not put on screen yet?

10. Close

  • 48Where should people watch Killer Revenge, and where do they follow you for the second one?
  • 49Julio Where can people find your work?
  • 50Both What do you want people to take away from it?
