THE SÉANCE

Chris R. Notarile · Blinky Productions · Krueger: Tales from Elm Street

Saturday October 10, 2026, 4:00 PM. In person, about ninety minutes at the table.

Chris, tick the ones you want and send back the numbers. Ignore anything you would rather not get into. We do not have to stick to the list on the day, and we can cut anything afterwards.

Two things so there are no surprises. There is a short yes/no round at the end: six questions, you answer only by sliding the planchette to YES or NO, one pass allowed. You will not see those ahead of time, that is the fun. And anything you and Landon have worked on together that is not announced yet stays off the table on air.

1. The opening

  • 1You didn't see a horror movie until you were 16 because your parents thought you'd get nightmares. Twenty-two years of giving other people nightmares later: what was the first one and what did it do to you?
  • 2In 2013 Marvel wiped 14 of your videos in a week and your reaction on record was basically "cost of doing business." Was that how it felt that night?
  • 3Why "Blinky"? It's on everything you've made since 2004 and the story is nowhere online.

2. Krueger and the craft

  • 4You put Roberto in the Freddy makeup yourself, three hours of latex by the finale. What did you learn about Freddy's face from applying it that you can't learn from watching it?
  • 5Chapter one is a ten-minute 1970s police interrogation with no dream powers at all. Why open a Freddy series by taking away the thing that makes him Freddy?
  • 6"Another Tale" has Freddy at a playground trying to get a girl into his car, the part of the character New Line has spent decades avoiding. Where was the line on set?
  • 7You told everyone chapter five was the end, then came back for a finale where Roberto plays himself and gets attacked by his own Freddy. What changed, and what do you owe the fans asking for one more?
  • 8You bought a replica glove online for chapter one and hated it. What's the one prop you'll never cheap out on again?
  • 9Roberto was a bit part in 2007 before he was Freddy. The petition to cast him in the reboot got 1,119 signatures. How close did that get to a real phone call?

3. No money

  • 10"As little as $10 or as much as $200" a short. What does the $10 version look like, and what's the cost nobody puts in that number?
  • 11Methodic, your first feature, you branded "the world's first basher movie." Creative idea or marketing move to get a no-budget slasher noticed?
  • 12You've offered finder's fees for investor intros and sold product placement. Which of those actually produced a dollar?
  • 13100,000 subscribers, 7 paying Patreon members. What does that gap teach anyone who thinks a subscriber count is a living?
  • 14A short a month since January 2024, 400 credits. What breaks first on that schedule?
  • 15In 2020 you made films that needed kids without kids on set, using sound and voice-over to imply them. Walk us through one scene the audience never clocked.

4. The legal line

  • 16Marvel pulled Deadpool after three episodes. Freddy has been on your channel fifteen years and New Line never touched him. What's the actual rule?
  • 17Fox legal wrote you back about Prey and you still didn't sue, because you'd rather pitch a script than burn a bridge. Did anyone at Disney or Fox ever call?
  • 18Miss Fury, The Black Hood, The Shadow, Lady Luck: all public domain. Artistic choice or legal strategy, and when did you make it?

5. Comics

  • 19JJ Flask came out as a photo-novella, then an audio drama, and the announced short never did. Why does a story live in one medium and die in another?
  • 20You trained in stop-motion because animators do everything themselves. How much of BCU Comics is that same instinct: can't get the budget to shoot it, draw it?
  • 21You've put a working actor sitting at this table into one of your comics. Without naming the book: what makes you look at a performer and think "that face is a panel, not a frame"?

6. Close

  • 22Where should people watch Krueger: Tales from Elm Street, and where do they follow you for what's next?
  • 23What do you want people to take away from twenty-two years of making these for nothing?
Reply with the numbers you want and we will build around them. Nothing here is fixed.
