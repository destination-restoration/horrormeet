Wednesday, October 28, 8:30 PM, at Danny's place in Laurel Canyon. About ninety minutes at the table.

Here are the questions we have for the two of you. Read them over and send Landon the numbers you love and any you don't want to touch. Nothing is fixed, and we can cut anything afterwards.

Near the end there is a quick yes-or-no lightning round on the Ouija board. We don't share those ahead of time, and you can answer out loud if you'd rather not touch the board.

It's casual. The point is for you to tell your story.

The opening

  • 1How did each of you end up making horror in Los Angeles? Sharon, you came out in 2018. Kyle, you came from Colorado.
  • 2How do a director and his lead actress decide to make movies together, and who has the final say on set?
  • 3What scared each of you as a kid, and does it still?

Inside the Asylum

  • 4Sharon, you have made more than a dozen films with The Asylum: sharks, a giant boa, Martians, a planet that quakes. How fast does one of those go from script to screen?
  • 5You played Joan of Arc in The Last Sharknado and doubled Cassie on second unit in the same film. What did doubling teach you about acting?
  • 6On Arctic Apocalypse you were in the background and second AD at the same time. What does a schedule like that ask of you?
  • 7Sgt. Mora in Monster Hunters, Esme West in Megalodon: The Frenzy, Tara Moto in Planetquake. How do you react to a creature that will not exist until the VFX team builds it?
  • 8End of the World was your first speaking role, and you used your own memories of earthquakes. Where does real fear come from when the monster is a green screen?
  • 9The titles are famous for sounding like a bigger movie. What do people get wrong about the Asylum, and what did it teach you that a bigger set would not?

Gush

  • 10Kyle, Gush started as a 48-hour competition film and cost about five thousand dollars. What did you have to give up to make it, and what did you refuse to give up?
  • 11A drug that brings three friends closer and then turns them on each other. Where did that idea come from?
  • 12Is it a ghost or is it their own heads? Did you ever decide, or is that the point?
  • 13Sharon, you are in front of the camera and a producer on Gush. What does a producer actually do on a 13-minute horror short?

Intrusive

  • 14Gush became the drug at the center of Intrusive. How does a short grow into a feature?
  • 15Ollie has lived with intrusive thoughts since he was a kid. How do you put the fear inside someone's head on screen?
  • 16Bob Cook has been making genre films since the eighties. Why hand him the director's chair on a script you wrote?
  • 17Sharon, you play Max. Who is she, and what is it like to read a part your partner wrote?

Making it in LA

  • 18Media Misfits makes horror, comedy and a Christmas movie. How do you pick what to make next?
  • 19Sharon, you studied psychology. Does knowing how fear works change how you play it?
  • 20Who are your horror heroes, and whose career do you want?

Close

  • 21What is next for each of you, and where should people find you?
  • 22One piece of advice for someone with a phone and a weekend who wants to make their first horror short.
