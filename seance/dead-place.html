THE SÉANCE

Michael Pickle + James Rivera · The Dead Place · Pay Up · Ghostman & Rivera's Horror Show

Sunday, November 22, at Landon's place in Laurel Canyon. Time to be confirmed (7:30 PM or earlier, your pick). About ninety minutes at the table.

Michael and James, here are the questions we have for you. Read them over and send Landon the numbers you love and any you don't want to touch. Nothing is fixed, and we can cut anything afterwards.

The Ouija board is part of the show. A few times we'll ask a yes-or-no question that you answer only by sliding the planchette, there's a quick lightning round near the end, and at one point you'll spell two letters. We don't share those questions ahead of time. Hold the planchette on your answer for a second so the camera above the board catches it. You get one pass, you can defend one answer after each round, and you can answer out loud any time you'd rather not touch the board.

Anything you are not cleared to talk about yet, like the next chapter of the trilogy, stays off the table. Tell Landon before we roll and it will not come up.

It's casual. Two podcasters who became filmmakers, a candlelit table, and the real story of how The Dead Place got made.

Where it started

  • 1Michael, a night at the old Rancho Los Amigos asylum in Downey is said to have ended your band and started your film career. Tell us that night.
  • 2James, you have been beside Michael for years. When did you first realize he was going to turn that night into a movie?
  • 3Before the movies there was Pickle's Horror Show on Roku. What did hosting teach you two about what horror fans actually want?

The $4,000 film school

  • 4Pay Up was shot in five and a half days for about $4,000. What did that shoot teach you that you carried into The Dead Place?
  • 5What is the first thing that goes wrong on a micro-budget set, every single time?
  • 6Who does what between you two? Where does a writer-director end and a producer begin on a film this size?

Building The Dead Place

  • 7How much of the film is what really happened to Michael, and where did you let the movie take over?
  • 8The Dead Place was cast in 2022 and came out in June 2026. What kept it alive through four years?
  • 9You crowdfunded part of it. What do people get wrong about raising money from fans?
  • 10Robert Bravo's practical effects are the thing reviewers keep praising. What was the hardest effect to pull off, and what did it cost you in time?

The New Kid and The Entity

  • 11David Howard Thornton sat on your podcast before he was cast. How did a podcast guest become The New Kid?
  • 12The world knows him as a silent Art the Clown. What did you want audiences to hear when you finally gave him a voice?
  • 13Michael, you called The New Kid talkative and sadistic. How did you and David build that character together?
  • 14Bill Oberst Jr. plays The Entity. How did you land him, and what did he bring that wasn't on the page?

Getting it seen

  • 15Why a free BloodStreamTV exclusive first, then a Beverly Hills premiere, then rental? Walk us through that decision.
  • 16The reviews were split, from two stars to four. Which criticism stung, and which one did you agree with?
  • 17What did the premiere night with David and the Q&A feel like after four years?

What's next

  • 18The Dead Place was pitched as part one of a trilogy. What can you tell us about where Isaac and Katharine go next?
  • 19If someone gave you ten times the budget tomorrow, what would you change about how you make a movie, and what would you refuse to change?
  • 20You still run Ghostman & Rivera's Horror Show. Has making films changed how you talk about other people's films?

Close

  • 21Where should people watch The Dead Place, where can they find you two, and what would you tell someone trying to make their first horror feature for almost nothing?
