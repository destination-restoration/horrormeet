THE SÉANCE

Adam Marcus · Director · Jason Goes to Hell: The Final Friday · Secret Santa

At Landon's place in Laurel Canyon. Date being set with Landon. About ninety minutes at the table.

Adam, here are the questions we have for you. Read them over and send Landon the numbers you love and any you don't want to touch. Nothing is fixed, and we can cut anything afterwards.

The Ouija board is part of the show. A few times we'll ask you a yes-or-no question that you answer only by sliding the planchette, there's a quick lightning round near the end, and at one point you'll spell two letters. We don't share those questions ahead of time. Hold the planchette on your answer for a second so the camera above the board catches it. You get one pass, you can defend one answer after each round, and you can answer out loud any time you'd rather not touch the board.

If you have anything from the film, the documentary or your artwork that you'd like on the table, bring it.

It's casual. You have told some of these stories before. We want the parts that don't usually fit.

The opening

  • 1You grew up in Westport, Connecticut. What was the first movie that truly scared you, and what did it do to you?
  • 2Before you ever directed, you were around film sets as a kid. What did you see up close that made you want the job?
  • 3You trained at NYU. What did film school get right, and what did you have to unlearn the first week of a real shoot?

The job at 23

  • 4You were Sean Cunningham's assistant, and you asked for a movie. How do you ask for that at that age?
  • 5The story goes that Bob Shaye at New Line gave you a horror exam before he would approve you. What was on it?
  • 6You have said the note you were handed was to get rid of the hockey mask. What did you think when you heard it?
  • 7You ran a crew of about a hundred people at 23. What did the first day feel like, and who did you lean on?

Jason Goes to Hell

  • 8Jason jumps from body to body in your film, and people are still arguing about it. Why was that the right story for you?
  • 9You have said your first version of the story was darker. What was in it that we never saw?
  • 10The Necronomicon is sitting in the Voorhees house. How did that book get onto your set?
  • 11The glove at the end launched a ten-year wait for Freddy vs. Jason. Whose idea was it, and what did you expect to happen next?
  • 12Which effect or stunt in that film were you sure would not work, right up until it did?

What came after

  • 13What happened in the weeks after opening weekend? Which rooms opened, and which ones closed?
  • 14You have said the audience in 1993 was warm and the anger came later, online. What is it like to watch a film's reputation change around you?
  • 15Thirty-three years on, you call yourself very proud of it. Which criticism do you now think was fair?

Chainsaw and the $30,000 movie

  • 16Your draft of Texas Chainsaw 3D was set in 1993, and it was rewritten after you left. Which scene do you most want back?
  • 17Secret Santa was shot for about $30,000 in eleven nights and a day. What did you learn making that film that New Line's money never taught you?
  • 18You have said Secret Santa was pirated and never made its money back. What should an indie horror filmmaker do differently today?
  • 19What is in your garage from thirty years of making monsters?

Now

  • 20You have run a theater troupe since 1995 and you train actors. What do you teach an actor about fear?
  • 21There is now a documentary about the making of your film, and you wanted the critics in it. Why?

Close

  • 22What are you making next, where should people find you and your artwork, and what would you tell a 23-year-old who has just been handed their first horror film?
