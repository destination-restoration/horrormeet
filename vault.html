THE VAULT
The public-domain masters · free to watch, forever, legally

These films built the genre, and their copyrights lapsed into the public domain, which means they belong to all of us now. Streams served by the Internet Archive. Watch them like you owe them something, because you do.

Night of the Living Dead

1968 · dir. George A. Romero

The modern zombie is invented in a Pittsburgh farmhouse for $114,000. A copyright filing error put it in the public domain, which is the only reason half of horror exists: everyone could study it for free.

▶ WATCH FREE

Nosferatu

1922 · dir. F.W. Murnau

The unauthorized Dracula a court ordered destroyed. It survives because the copies had already spread too far to catch: which is also why our first hundred members are called Surviving Print.

▶ WATCH FREE

Carnival of Souls

1962 · dir. Herk Harvey

An industrial-film director from Kansas makes one feature, on a whim, for $33,000, and accidentally invents the American art-horror film. The organ score alone is worth the visit.

▶ WATCH FREE

House on Haunted Hill

1959 · dir. William Castle

Vincent Price offers five strangers $10,000 each to survive a night in a haunted house. Yes, we are aware our bounty is cheaper. Inflation is a horror of its own.

▶ WATCH FREE

The Last Man on Earth

1964 · dir. Ubaldo Ragona & Sidney Salkow

Vincent Price, alone against a world of the infected: the first film of I Am Legend, and the blueprint Romero openly borrowed for Night of the Living Dead.

▶ WATCH FREE

White Zombie

1932 · dir. Victor Halperin

Bela Lugosi, one year after Dracula, in the first zombie feature ever made: back when the word meant something older and stranger than hunger.

▶ WATCH FREE

The Terror

1963 · dir. Roger Corman

Corman had the castle set for two more days, so he shot another movie in it. With Boris Karloff and a very young Jack Nicholson. That is the whole origin story, and it is perfect.
